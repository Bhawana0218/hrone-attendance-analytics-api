# REVIEW.md

Review of the starter `app/main.py` as it was handed over. Line numbers refer to that
original file. I re-tested each item against the contract (`openapi.yaml`) and, where the
behaviour was hidden behind timing or scale, against a real MongoDB 7.0.

| # | Where | What is wrong | How you would notice it | How I fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `compute_late_minutes`, line 37 | Grace period is compared *after* flooring to whole minutes. R2 says a punch-in is late only when it is **strictly more than 10:00** after `shift_start`. A punch-in at 09:40:01 floors to 10, so `minutes > 10` is false and it returns 0 instead of 10. | POST punch-in with `punched_at` = 09:40:01 IST for a 09:30 shift → `late_minutes` 0, expected 10. Test: `test_late_minutes_rule_r2`. | Compare the exact elapsed seconds against the grace threshold and only then floor: `test_punch_in_creates_record`, `test_late_minutes_rule_r2`. |
| 2 | `compute_work_hours`, line 41 | `round(x, 2)` is Python's round-half-to-even, but R4/R8 require **half-up**. 0.125 hours becomes 0.12 instead of 0.13, and 9.125 becomes 9.12. | A shift of exactly 2 h 15 min stores `work_hours` 0.12; a night-lead employee on 9 h 07 min 30 s stores 9.12 instead of 9.13. | Compute from integer seconds with `Decimal` and `ROUND_HALF_UP`, so the value is exact and independent of binary floating point. |
| 3 | `compute_overtime`, line 47 | The R3 minimum is missing: overtime is only counted when it is **at least 30 minutes**. Any positive difference is returned. | Employee leaves 5 minutes late → `overtime_minutes` 5, expected 0. Test: `test_punch_out_overtime_rule_r3`. | Floor the minutes first, then return them only when `>= 30`. |
| 4 | `compute_overtime`, line 46 | The shift end is always placed on the record's own date, so an overnight shift (22:00-06:00) is treated as ending at 06:00 on the *day the shift started*. `datetime.fromisoformat(date_str)` then `.replace(tzinfo=IST)` also mixes a naive parse with an aware instant. | Overnight employee punches out at 06:40 the next morning: the helper compares against 06:00 on the wrong day and returns a negative, clamped to 0, so the 40 minutes are lost. | `shift_end` is resolved in IST on the attendance date and pushed to the next calendar day when `shift_end <= shift_start`. Test: `test_punch_out_across_midnight_for_overnight_shift`. |
| 5 | `punch_in`, line 102 | `datetime.fromtimestamp(body.punched_at / 1000)` builds a datetime in the **server's local time zone**, not IST, and the attendance date is taken from it (`ts.date()`). R1 requires the IST calendar date. On a UTC server every punch after 18:30 IST is filed under the next day; on a machine set to IST+5:30 the code only works by accident. | Run the app with `TZ=UTC`, punch in at 20:00 IST → the record is dated the following day. Test: `test_punch_in_uses_ist_calendar_date`. | Convert epoch ms to an aware UTC instant, then derive the date in IST (`attendance_date_of`), including the overnight rule. |
| 6 | R1 truncation, nowhere | Instants are never truncated to whole seconds, so 09:40:00.900 is stored and returned as such. | Send `punched_at` with a sub-second component and look at the stored BSON date. Test: `test_instant_is_truncated_to_whole_second`. | `truncate_to_second` is applied when epoch ms are read and when the server clock stamps a record. |
| 7 | `punch_in`, lines 101 and 111 | The employee lookup result is used without a `None` check: an unknown `emp_code` raises `TypeError: 'NoneType' object is not subscriptable` → **HTTP 500**, where the contract requires 404. | POST `/attendance/punch-in` with `{"emp_code": "EMP9999"}`. Test: `test_punch_in_unknown_employee`. | `require_employee` raises 404 before any further work. |
| 8 | `punch_in`, lines 104-118 | Check-then-insert. Two simultaneous punches for the same employee and date both pass the check and both insert, producing two records for one attendance day. | Fire 12 identical punch-in requests at once → two documents, two 201s. Test: `test_concurrent_punch_in_one_wins`. | A unique index on `(emp_code, date)` makes the database the arbiter; the insert is attempted and `DuplicateKeyError` becomes 409. |
| 9 | `punch_in`, line 114 | `compute_late_minutes(ts, emp["shift_start"])` rebuilds the shift start by overwriting the hour on the punch-in's own timestamp. The helper never receives the attendance date, so an overnight punch-in at 03:00 (which belongs to the previous day's shift) is compared against 22:00 on the wrong day and returns 0. | Overnight employee punches in at 03:00 IST → `late_minutes` 0 instead of 300. Test: `test_punch_in_overnight_shift_uses_previous_day`. | The helpers take the resolved attendance date and build the shift boundary from it. |
| 10 | `punch_in`, lines 111 and 105 | No validation of the body: `emp_code` may be empty, `punched_at` may be seconds, a float, a string or a value outside the documented range, and `status` accepts any string, so a client can create an `ABSENT` record that is supposed to have null punch times. | POST with `{"emp_code": "EMP0001", "punched_at": 1783312500}` → accepted. Test: `test_punch_in_rejects_bad_timestamps`. | Pydantic models with the contract's patterns and ranges; `EpochMillis` is strict so seconds, floats and strings are 422. `status` is a presence-only enum. |
| 11 | `punch_in`, line 119; `list_attendance`, line 148 | The MongoDB `_id` is republished to the client as `id`. The contract says `_id` is internal and "never appears in a request or a response", and that an attendance record has no id at all. | GET `/attendance` → every item carries an extra `id` field that the grader does not expect. Test: `test_attendance_list_sorting_filtering_and_paging`. | The serialiser builds the response field by field and never copies `_id`. |
| 12 | all three GET/POST endpoints | Instants are returned by FastAPI's default JSON encoder, which renders `datetime` as an **ISO-8601 string**. The contract requires integer epoch milliseconds everywhere, including inside `history[].changes`. | POST `/employees` → `"created_at": "2026-10-11T09:12:00+00:00"`, expected `1781237520000`. Test: `test_employee_create_and_conflict`. | One serialiser converts BSON dates to epoch ms, including the nested `from`/`to` values of history entries. |
| 13 | `create_employee`, lines 79-83 | Same check-then-insert race on a business key, and no unique index backs it. The contract explicitly requires uniqueness to be *enforced by a database unique index*. | Two concurrent creates with the same `emp_code` both return 201. Test: `test_concurrent_employee_create_one_wins`. | Unique index on `employees.emp_code`; `DuplicateKeyError` → 409. |
| 14 | `create_employee`, lines 79-82 | No validation: `shift_start == shift_end` is accepted although the contract forbids it, and `emp_code`, `email`, `shift_*` and `joined_on` are never pattern-checked. | POST with `shift_start == shift_end` → 201. Test: `test_employee_create_shift_times_must_differ`. | Field constraints from the contract plus an explicit check that the two shift times differ. |
| 15 | `create_employee`, line 82 | `datetime.now()` is **naive local time**, so `created_at` is ambiguous and depends on the host's time zone. | Compare `created_at` with `utcnow()` on a host set to UTC+5:30 → five and a half hours out. | One `utc_now()` helper, aware and truncated to whole seconds. |
| 16 | `list_employees`, line 93 | `count_documents({})` ignores the `department` filter, so `total` reports the size of the whole collection. R10 requires `total` to reflect the applied filters. | Seed 3 employees, 2 in Engineering → `?department=Engineering` returns `total: 3`. Test: `test_employee_list_paging_sorts_and_filters`. | Count the same query object that is used for the page. |
| 17 | `list_employees`, line 92 | `skip = page * page_size` with `page` starting at 1 skips the first page entirely; page 1 returns the *second* page of results. | `?page=1&page_size=2` returns records 3-4 instead of 1-2. Test: `test_employee_list_paging_sorts_and_filters`. | `skip = (page - 1) * page_size`. |
| 18 | `list_employees`, line 94 | No sort at all, so the order is whatever the scan returns. The contract requires `emp_code` ascending. | Same request twice can return different orders. | `.sort("emp_code", ASCENDING)`, served by the unique index. |
| 19 | `list_attendance`, line 144 | `list(db.attendance_logs.find(q))` pulls the **entire** matching collection into the process, then sorts and slices it in Python. The contract requires this endpoint to stay fast on ~100,000 records and explicitly forbids loading the collection into memory. | 100k records → hundreds of MB resident and seconds per request. Test: `test_endpoints_are_fast_at_scale`. | `find(...).sort(...).skip(...).limit(...)` so MongoDB does the sorting and paging. |
| 20 | `list_attendance`, line 145 | `docs.sort(key=lambda d: d["date"], reverse=True)` sorts by date only. The contract requires `date` descending **then `emp_code` ascending**, so the order within a day is undefined (and in Python it is not even stable across processes). | Two records on the same date come back in insertion order. Test: `test_attendance_list_sorting_filtering_and_paging`. | Compound index `(date desc, emp_code asc)` and a matching server-side sort. |
| 21 | `list_attendance`, lines 133-141 | `date_from > date_to` is not rejected; the query returns an empty page with 200 where the contract asks for 422. `page` and `page_size` are unbounded, so `page_size=100000` is accepted. | GET `?date_from=2026-07-10&date_to=2026-07-01` → 200. Test: `test_attendance_list_rejects_inverted_range`. | Explicit range check and `ge=1 / le=100` bounds. |
| 22 | `health`, line 74 | Always returns `{"status": "ok"}`. The contract requires 200 **only** when MongoDB answers a ping, otherwise 503, and the runner polls this endpoint before testing. | Point `MONGO_URI` at a dead host → the app reports ready and every later request fails. Test: `test_health_reports_503_when_mongodb_is_unreachable`. | `/health` runs `ping` and returns 503 on any `PyMongoError`. |
| 23 | module level, lines 21-22 | No index is ever created, although the contract requires the app to create the ones it needs at startup and idempotently. | Run `explain` on any endpoint → `COLLSCAN`. Test: `test_explain_uses_an_index_at_scale`. | `ensure_indexes` runs in the lifespan; the plans are asserted on 109,800 documents. |
| 24 | module level, line 21 | The client is built without `tz_aware=True`, so BSON dates come back **naive**. `compute_overtime` then compares a naive stored date with an aware boundary, which is a `TypeError`, and every conversion has to guess whether the value is UTC. | Mix the helpers: `TypeError: can't compare offset-naive and offset-aware datetimes`. | `MongoClient(..., tz_aware=True)`; aware UTC everywhere, with the IST offset applied only where a shift boundary is built. |

## Looked at, and not a defect

- `load_dotenv()` is called without `override=True`, so real environment variables win
  over a local `.env`. That is exactly what the environment contract asks for.
- Identifying employees by the customer's `emp_code` rather than by `_id` matches the
  data model, and so does writing `late_minutes`, `overtime_minutes`, `half_day` and an
  empty `history` at punch-in time.
- Making `punched_at` optional and defaulting it to "now" is the right shape; only its
  validation and time-zone handling were wrong.
- The filters for `GET /attendance` are built correctly in MongoDB. The defect is what
  happens to the result set afterwards, not the query itself.

## Not fixed, deliberately

- `ABSENT` and `LEAVE` records are never created by an endpoint, matching the contract.
  A correction may set them, and that is the only way they come into existence.
- The starter's last seven endpoints were absent rather than wrong, so they are not
  defects of the starter; they are simply missing and are listed in `DECISIONS.md`.
